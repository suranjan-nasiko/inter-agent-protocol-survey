The 2024 to 2026 period produced an explosion of inter-agent protocols: Google
A2A, Anthropic MCP, IBM ACP, Cisco AGNTCY/SLIM, ANP, Coral, LOKA, ACNBP, AP2,
Visa TAP, ERC-8004, and UCP each address fragments of one coordination
problem. Four surveys exist. What is missing is a basis for comparing them.

This survey contributes a uniform rubric of ten axes: discovery, identity,
authorization, transport, task model, async, multi-modal handling, security
threat surface, semantic interoperability, and governance. We cover thirteen
protocols across three layers: tool-binding, peer-messaging, and
commerce-settlement. The eight tool-binding and peer-messaging protocols are
scored on the full rubric. The five commerce-settlement protocols are compared
structurally, since the task, transport, and threat axes do not apply to a
settlement envelope. Scores come from one rater reading each specification
under stated calibration rules. We report an auditable change history over a
machine-readable score file, and no inter-rater agreement statistic. We map
adoption across enterprise SaaS, agentic commerce, healthcare, and finance,
and survey the security literature.

We then identify the unresolved problems that should drive the next
standardization cycle. From that agenda we propose three extensions. OWA is a
URI scoping scheme, adding a workspace tier between the organizational trust
root and the agent identifier. ACSP is a sibling envelope recording the
sender's declared context-selection step: which candidate items were
considered, which kept, which dropped, under which token budget and objective.
AGRP is a normative A2A envelope binding every cross-boundary message to a
classification-label set and a commitment-bearing redaction log. The three
close one loop, naming to selection to attestation, that no current protocol
provides. All three are specification sketches, not validated standards.
